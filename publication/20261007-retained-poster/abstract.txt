A compact representation is useful only if it preserves task relevant variation. We study this distinction in LAM JEPA, a hashed token model with a vector quantizer, sparse memory, a latent action rollout, and target encoder alignment. The evaluated encoder mean pools embeddings; its target receives the same input. It is not a Transformer or canonical prediction of a distinct context target.

A frozen ARC Challenge validation protocol compared the full model with a gradient active parameter matched supervised baseline and planner and target ablations across five seeds. Full accuracy was 0.2549 versus 0.2664 for the matched baseline, a paired difference of negative 0.0115. The declared superiority criterion required at least positive 0.02 with a paired confidence interval excluding zero. Planner and target attribution criteria also failed. Reviewed quantized runs used one code and produced constant predictions. Removing quantization restored input dependence in a bounded diagnostic without establishing above chance accuracy.

We present the retained adverse evidence, a diagnostic separation between representation compression and collapse, and limits on mechanism attribution. A trainability repair did not establish generalization or quantization benefit. The protected confirmatory test remains unopened for this failed hypothesis. Five seeds and a collapsed comparison regime do not support general conclusions about planning, quantization, or joint embedding methods.

The contribution is an auditable failure case with matched controls and preserved decision rules. For parsimony research, it illustrates why code utilization, output diversity, task performance, and a positive control must be inspected together before interpreting a compact representation as useful.
